# 11 — Revised evidence-based story

Reviewed 15 October 2026 · Task 02 · Author: Codex (technical audit). No visualisations implemented.

Retain **Malaysia's Hospital Divide** as a working title, with a scope subtitle such as **Public and private capacity, reported activity and demographic change**. State the actual windows: public capacity 2015–2024, private comparisons/activity2023–2024, public workforce 2014–2022, and facility register 31 December 2025. These are historical observations, not a live 2026 assessment.

A stronger central question is now: **How has hospital capacity kept pace with population, how does private provision change regional comparisons, and what does reported MOH activity add to that picture?** Annual utilisation can enter the narrative. Current ICU/non-ICU pressure and realised access remain unresolved.

1. **National public capacity grew faster than population.** Retain Task 01's verified 2015–2024 bed growth 15.44% versus 9.19% population growth. Seven states/territories still had declining public rates. An indexed national opening followed by a state-year view establishes the aggregate and its variation.
2. **Private supply changes what a regional “divide” means.** Introduce the 2024 state comparison:19,920 private hospital beds; Pulau Pinang 15th→4th and Selangor 16th→10th when adding them to public beds. Nearly 60% of private beds sit in Selangor, Kuala Lumpur and Pulau Pinang. Pair rate comparison with geography after boundary review. Explain that licensed private beds do not establish affordable access and special public institutions have different care roles.
3. **Official capacity, operational beds and reported activity are different measures.** MOH reports 46,855 official versus 44,570 operational beds and 72.61% annual BOR in 2024. Introduce state occupancy and admissions without an “overload” threshold. Government beyond MOH and private care are outside this BOR scope. Submission/response rates limit demand-change interpretations. Private admissions include more institution types than private hospital capacity. Avoid a current-pressure headline from daily KKMNOW anomalies.
4. **Ageing and workforce add context without becoming need estimates.** Retain national 65+share 5.84%→7.66% for 2015–2024. Public doctor and nurse headcounts grew differently through 2022; use state-year matched rates, while saying these cover healthcare workers generally, not hospital shifts or FTE. Do not combine 2022 staff with 2024 beds to imply staffing adequacy.
5. **Conclude with differences that survive the definitions.** National growth coexists with state variation; private supply changes ranking; available/operational concepts differ; recorded activity reflects provider mix and reporting. The evidence supports a distribution story. It does not establish shortages, equitable access or the causes of regional differences.
